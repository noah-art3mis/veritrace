# Standard fact-check representations and how VERITRACE maps onto them

Research for issue #121. Surveys the load-bearing representations the field uses – FActScore/SAFE, ClaimReview (schema.org), the Google Fact Check Tools API, AVeriTeC, FEVER/FEVEROUS, ClaimBuster, ClaimsKG, MediaReview, IFCN, stance detection – and maps VERITRACE's bespoke graph schema (`lib/graph-types.ts`) onto each: where we align, where we silently diverge, and what emitting or consuming them natively would buy. The survey literature is folded in where it bears on the schema: the Guo/Schlichtkrull/Vlachos pipeline frame opens §1, the decomposition alignment-bottleneck result extends §1.2, Ev2R extends the AVeriTeC-score material in §1.5, and the claim-detection and stance-detection surveys extend §1.7 and §1.11. Code citations are `file:line` against the repo at the time of writing; external claims cite the primary source (vendored under `docs/papers/`).

## 1. The representations

**The pipeline frame.** The field's standard decomposition, per the canonical survey (Guo, Schlichtkrull & Vlachos, _A Survey on Automated Fact-Checking_, TACL 10:178–206, 2022, [doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00454](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00454); vendored at `docs/papers/guo-2022-automated-fact-checking-survey.pdf`), is three stages: **claim detection** (which claims need checking – framed either as subjective _check-worthiness_ per Hassan et al. 2015 or as objective _checkability_ per Konstantinovskiy et al. 2021, binary or as an importance ranking mirroring journalistic triage), **evidence retrieval**, and **claim verification**, the last splitting into **verdict prediction** and **justification production**. Two of the survey's framing choices are load-bearing for our schema. First, stance detection "can be viewed as an instantiation of evidence retrieval" that "assumes a more limited amount of potential evidence and predicts its stance towards the claim" – which is exactly where `EvidenceItem.stance` sits (see §1.11). Second, on labels: when evidence is used, "it is often preferable to use supported/refuted (by evidence) instead of true/false respectively, as in many cases the evidence itself is not assessed by the systems" – the rationale behind our `supported`/`refuted` naming, and the same epistemic modesty as the advisory-verdict posture. VERITRACE's stages map cleanly: decompose + triage (`checkworthy`/`relevant`/`relevanceScore`) = claim detection; question generation + search = evidence retrieval; the stated verdict rules = verdict prediction; `rationale` + the visible `QuestionTrace` = justification production. The survey also names the `claims:search` short-circuit's task: _detecting previously fact-checked claims_ (Shaar et al. 2020), which it notes "can often be reduced to sentence-level similarity" but warns that "previous fact-checks can be useful, but they can become outdated and potentially misleading over time" – a caveat our short-circuit inherits wholesale.

### 1.1 FActScore

**What it encodes:** a long-form generation decomposed into _atomic facts_, each judged against a reliable knowledge source (Wikipedia in the paper). **Label vocabulary:** binary per fact – supported / not-supported. **Aggregate:** FActScore = the percentage of atomic facts supported by the knowledge source (0–100%); the automated estimator (retrieval + a strong LM) tracks human annotation within <2% error. Source: Min et al., _FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation_, EMNLP 2023, [arXiv:2305.14251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251).

FActScore's `postprocess_atomic_facts()` also _auto-corrects_ decontextualization: it detects entities in the atomic fact absent from the source (spaCy NER) and string-replaces them back to the source's surface form. VERITRACE deliberately does the detection half only, emitting an advisory `injected` list instead of mutating the claim – documented with rationale at `lib/pipeline/audit.ts:14-33` (human-in-the-loop; injection is not always an error; string-replace is unsound across translation). This is the #15 divergence the issue references.

### 1.2 SAFE / F1@K

**What it encodes:** the successor pattern to FActScore for open-web checking – an LLM agent splits a response into individual facts, revises each to be self-contained, then rates it via multi-step Google Search reasoning. **Label vocabulary:** 3-way per fact – supported / irrelevant / not-supported. **Aggregate:** F1@K, which balances precision (fraction of supported facts) against recall relative to a hyperparameter K (the user's preferred number of facts). Source: Wei et al., _Long-form factuality in large language models_, [arXiv:2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802). VERITRACE's decompose-then-check pipeline and the `ClaimTally` "X of N supported" signal are explicitly SAFE-derived (`lib/pipeline/verdict.ts:119-124` cites "SAFE F1@K"; `lib/graph-types.ts:103`), and SAFE's _relevance_ axis is mirrored in `ClaimItem.relevant` (`lib/graph-types.ts:82-89`).

**The alignment bottleneck.** The decompose-then-check pattern itself now has a boundary condition. Akhter et al., _The Alignment Bottleneck in Decomposition-Based Claim Verification_, [arXiv:2602.10380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10380) (vendored at `docs/papers/akhter-2026-alignment-bottleneck.pdf`), test decomposition on real-world complex claims with human-annotated sub-claim evidence spans, under two setups: **Sub-claim Aligned Evidence** (SAE – each sub-claim paired with its own evidence span) and **Repeated Claim-level Evidence** (SRE – every sub-claim sees the same undifferentiated claim-level evidence pool). The finding: "decomposition brings significant performance improvement only when evidence is granular and strictly aligned"; SRE "fail[s] to improve and often degrade[s] performance" across PHEMEPlus, MMM-Fact, and COVID-Fact. And under noisy (predicted rather than oracle) sub-claim labels, the _error profile_ of the sub-claim verifier matters more than its accuracy: "conservative 'abstention' significantly reduces error propagation compared to aggressive but incorrect predictions", and "sub-claim macro-F1 is a poor predictor of success". Their conclusion is that "the primary bottleneck for deployment in real-world settings is not decomposition itself, but the capability for precise evidence synthesis and alignment", and that predictors should favor "calibrated abstention over incorrect polarity".

Three consequences for the decompose step (#52/#73) and `ClaimTally`. (a) VERITRACE is on the SAE side of the dichotomy _by construction_: evidence is retrieved per `QuestionItem` per `ClaimItem`, not gathered once per source and replayed against every claim – the paper is direct empirical support for that shape, and a warning against any "one retrieval pass for the whole paste" optimization that would quietly move us to SRE. (b) Our verdict rules' abstention bias – mixed deciding evidence resolves to NEI (`lib/pipeline/verdict.ts:64-67`), and the stated-not-learned rules generally prefer NEI to a forced polarity – is exactly the "calibrated abstention" the paper recommends; their error analysis also names the cost (their conservative model "almost never detects refutations"), so the bias is a trade we should state, not a free win. (c) Their limitations section flags a label-space mismatch – claim-level prediction restricted to {T,F} while sub-claims carry {T,F,U} – "can obscure how abstention should propagate to the final claim verdict"; VERITRACE avoids this trap because the 4-way vocabulary is uniform from `ClaimItem.verdict` through `ClaimTally` to `sourceVerdict`, so abstention propagates explicitly rather than being squeezed out at aggregation.

### 1.3 ClaimReview (schema.org)

**What it encodes:** the de-facto publication/interchange markup for a finished fact-check. Type chain `Thing → CreativeWork → Review → ClaimReview`; core properties: `claimReviewed` (text of the claim), `itemReviewed` (a `Claim`, with its own `author` = the claimant and `appearance`/`firstAppearance` = where the claim ran), `reviewRating` (a `Rating`), plus inherited `author` (the fact-checking org), `datePublished`, `url`. **Label vocabulary:** none imposed – `Rating.alternateName` carries the publisher's own label ("True", "Pants on Fire", "Distorcido"), while `ratingValue`/`bestRating`/`worstRating` optionally place it on a numeric scale. Sources: [schema.org/ClaimReview](https://schema.org/ClaimReview), Google's markup guide [developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/factcheck](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/factcheck) (which marks `alternateName` required and `ratingValue`/`bestRating`/`worstRating` recommended).

The crucial structural point for us: ClaimReview ratings are **dual-encoded** – free text (`alternateName`) _and_ a bounded numeric scale (`ratingValue` on `worstRating..bestRating`). VERITRACE currently reads only the free-text half (see §1.4).

### 1.4 Google Fact Check Tools API

**What it offers:** two surfaces – (a) the **Claim Search API** (`claims:search`, the endpoint VERITRACE calls at `lib/factcheck.ts:17`), querying the ClaimReview corpus behind Fact Check Explorer, and (b) the **ClaimReview Read/Write API** (`pages`) for publishers to manage their own markup. Docs: [developers.google.com/fact-check/tools/api](https://developers.google.com/fact-check/tools/api). Related corpora: the Data Commons ClaimReview downloads – a historical research dataset plus a daily-refreshed feed of markup created via the Fact Check Markup Tool / Read-Write API ([datacommons.org/factcheck/download](https://datacommons.org/factcheck/download)).

**Deprecation status (verified against Google's own pages, 2026-08):** the API itself carries **no deprecation or shutdown notice** – the API docs page is notice-free, and both endpoints remain documented. What _was_ deprecated is the **Search feature**: Google's structured-data guide states verbatim "We're phasing out support for `ClaimReview` markup in Google Search. However, this markup remains supported by the Factcheck Explorer Tool" ([source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/factcheck)). Press coverage dates the rich-result removal to June 2025 ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/06/google-kills-the-fact-checking-snippet/), [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/ifcn/2025/google-claimreview-fact-checks-snippets-removed/)). Practical read: the `claims:search` short-circuit and the ADR 0002 escape hatch (`docs/adr/0002-...md`, "the free Google Fact Check Tools API … within ToS") still stand, but the ecosystem _incentive_ for publishers to keep emitting ClaimReview markup has weakened – the corpus's freshness is now a risk to monitor, and the API has sat at `v1alpha1` since launch, which is its own maintenance-status signal.

Note the wire format: the API returns `claim.text`, `claim.claimant`, and per-review `publisher`, `url`, `title`, `reviewDate`, `textualRating`, `languageCode` ([reference](https://developers.google.com/fact-check/tools/api/reference/rest/v1alpha1/claims/search)) – i.e. it serves `Rating.alternateName` as `textualRating` and does **not** expose `ratingValue`/`bestRating`/`worstRating`. So the structured half of the rating is available in the underlying ClaimReview markup and the Data Commons feeds, but _not_ through `claims:search`. That reframes the issue's "we throw away structured rating scales" point: the throwing-away happens partly at Google's API boundary, not only in our `ratingToStance` (`lib/factcheck.ts:91-99`).

### 1.5 AVeriTeC

**What it encodes:** 4,568 real-world claims (50 fact-check orgs), each with **question–answer pairs** whose answers are backed by web evidence, a **verdict**, and a textual **justification** explaining how the QA pairs combine. Built explicitly to avoid context dependence, evidence insufficiency, and temporal leakage. **Label vocabulary:** exactly four – _Supported_, _Refuted_, _Not Enough Evidence_, _Conflicting Evidence/Cherry-picking_ (κ = 0.619). **Aggregate/eval:** the **AVeriTeC score** – verdict accuracy _conditioned_ on evidence quality: generated Q+A pairs are matched to gold ones by Hungarian-algorithm-optimal METEOR (hu-METEOR); if the Q+A match is below 0.25 the claim scores 0 regardless of the verdict; at most 10 QA pairs count. Sources: Schlichtkrull et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, [arXiv:2305.13117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117); dataset page [fever.ai/dataset/averitec.html](https://fever.ai/dataset/averitec.html) (CC-BY-NC-4.0); shared-task paper [arXiv:2410.23850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23850) / [ACL Anthology 2024.fever-1.1](https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.1/) for the metric definition.

This is VERITRACE's closest structural relative: `Source → ClaimItem → QuestionItem → EvidenceItem` is AVeriTeC's claim → QA-pairs → evidence with a document layer on top, and the 4-way `Verdict` union (`lib/graph-types.ts:4`) uses AVeriTeC's exact label set (ADR 0002 chose the golds partly _because_ "AVeriTeC already normalised to our exact 4 labels"). One deliberate semantic divergence: ADR 0007 restricts `conflicting` to the **document level** (cherrypicking across claims); a single atomic claim with mixed deciding evidence resolves to NEI (`lib/pipeline/verdict.ts:64-67`). AVeriTeC's _Conflicting Evidence/Cherry-picking_ label, by contrast, is a per-claim label in the dataset – ADR 0007 reads mixed-evidence-on-one-proposition as AVeriTeC's NEI, which is a defensible but _interpretive_ alignment, not a mechanical one. Eval comparisons on AVeriTeC golds should remember our pipeline can never predict per-claim `conflicting` while the gold set contains it. The Guo survey's "Choice of Labels" challenge lends ADR 0007 independent support: it observes that in-between labels like "mostly true" "often represent 'meta-ratings' for composite claims consisting of multiple elementary claims of different veracity" (their example: a politician's joint unemployment-and-productivity claim, half right, rated "half true") – i.e., the field's own diagnosis is that mixedness typically lives at the composite/document level, which is exactly where ADR 0007 puts `conflicting`.

**Ev2R – the successor to the hu-METEOR gate.** The AVeriTeC authors themselves have since replaced token matching for evidence evaluation: Akhtar, Schlichtkrull & Vlachos, _Ev2R: Evaluating Evidence Retrieval in Automated Fact-Checking_, [arXiv:2411.05375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05375) (vendored at `docs/papers/ev2r-2025-evidence-retrieval-eval.pdf`). Their critique of the status quo names all three prior evaluation modes: implicit evaluation through the predicted verdict (accurate-verdict-implies-good-evidence), exact match against a closed knowledge source (FEVER-style), and token-matching metrics like METEOR for open-web datasets – the last being "highly sensitive to surface form differences" and blind to _alternative evidence paths_ (two different QA chains can both validly establish the same verdict). **The scorer:** a weighted combination of (i) a **reference-based** component – an LLM judge decomposes both retrieved and reference evidence into atomic facts (FActScore-style, explicitly "inspired by Min et al. (2023)") and computes support-based precision (retrieved facts supported by the reference) and recall (reference facts covered by the retrieved evidence), combined as F1 – and (ii) a **proxy-reference** component – a DeBERTa-v3 NLI model fine-tuned on FEVER/VitaminC/HOVER/AVeriTeC emits its softmax confidence in the _gold_ verdict given the retrieved evidence – mixed with α = 0.5. **Meta-evaluation:** against human ratings and adversarial perturbations across AVeriTeC, FEVER, and VitaminC, Ev2R "consistently outperforms existing scoring approaches", with the gap over ROUGE-L/BLEU/METEOR/hu-METEOR "particularly prevalent on AVeriTeC, which includes real-world claims and evidence retrieved from the web". For VERITRACE this restructures the eval recommendation in §2.3: an AVeriTeC-style harness over `GoldenQuestion.keyEvidenceUrls` should gate on an Ev2R-style atomic-fact precision/recall judge, not on the shared-task hu-METEOR@0.25 – hu-METEOR is now a deprecated baseline by the metric's own authors, and the alternative-evidence-paths problem it fails on is precisely our live situation (open-web retrieval with no closed evidence universe). Note also the two components' independence: the proxy-reference half needs only `(claim, verdict, evidence)` – all of which a VERITRACE run and its golds already carry – so a proxy-only Ev2R variant is implementable without any reference-evidence annotation beyond what `keyEvidenceUrls` gives us.

### 1.6 FEVER / FEVEROUS

**FEVER:** 185,445 synthetic claims (altered Wikipedia sentences); **labels:** 3-way – _Supported / Refuted / NotEnoughInfo_ (Fleiss κ = 0.6841); Supported/Refuted claims carry the gold evidence _sentences_. **Aggregate:** the FEVER score – label correct **and** a complete gold evidence set retrieved (the paper's baseline: 31.87% with evidence required vs 50.91% label-only). Source: Thorne et al., NAACL 2018, [arXiv:1803.05355](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355). **FEVEROUS:** 87,026 claims extending evidence to **structured data** – table cells as well as sentences – same 3-way labels; the FEVEROUS score again requires correct evidence + verdict (baseline 18%). Source: Aly et al., [arXiv:2106.05707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05707).

FEVER matters to VERITRACE mostly as ancestry: our `Verdict` minus `conflicting` _is_ the FEVER label set, our `nei` takes FEVER's name, and the "evidence must be retrieved, not just the label right" scoring philosophy is what `GoldenGold.keyEvidenceUrls` (`evals/golden/schema.ts:59`) exists to support. FEVER's synthetic Wikipedia-only claims make it a poor gold source for us (ADR 0002 picked AVeriTeC for exactly the reasons FEVER falls short: artificial claims, temporal leakage).

### 1.7 ClaimBuster

**What it encodes:** _check-worthiness_ of individual sentences – a screening step before any verification. **Vocabulary:** 3 classes – NFS (non-factual), UFS (unimportant factual), CFS (check-worthy factual); the deployed system emits a continuous **check-worthiness score in [0, 1]** (the classifier's CFS probability) used to rank sentences. Trained on 20k+ human-labeled US-debate sentences. Source: Hassan et al., _Toward Automated Fact-Checking: Detecting Check-worthy Factual Claims by ClaimBuster_, KDD 2017, [PDF](https://ranger.uta.edu/~cli/pubs/2017/claimbuster-kdd17-hassan.pdf) / [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3097983.3098131).

VERITRACE re-derives this pipeline stage with three separate fields on `ClaimItem`: `checkworthy` (subjective/opinion/prediction → not check-worthy, `lib/graph-types.ts:80-81` – ClaimBuster's NFS), `relevant` (trivial presupposition → factual but not load-bearing, `:82-89` – close cousin of UFS), and the continuous `relevanceScore` (0..1 triage ranking driving `capSearchable`, `:90-96` – the direct analogue of ClaimBuster's score). Our decomposition is finer than ClaimBuster's (we also carry `checkable` for provenance-type claims the text-only build can't check, `:79`), but the score semantics differ subtly: ClaimBuster's number is a _check-worthiness probability_; our `relevanceScore` is a _load-bearingness/contestedness_ ranking used for capping. They correlate but are not the same quantity – worth remembering if we ever report "check-worthiness" numbers against ClaimBuster baselines.

The current claim-detection survey (Panchendrarajan & Zubiaga, _Claim detection for automated fact-checking_, NLP Journal 7:100066, 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.nlp.2024.100066](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlp.2024.100066); vendored at `docs/papers/panchendrarajan-2024-claim-detection-survey.pdf`) organizes the whole stage into **three factors – verifiability, priority, similarity** – with a three-stage refinement of "claim": _claim_ (a purported fact about the real world, vs opinion) → _verifiable claim_ ("a factual statement that can be checked"; personal experience and subjective statements excluded; crucially, "verifiable claim detection tasks often ignore the availability of evidence", since that can't be known until retrieval) → _check-worthy claim_ (the subjective prioritization layer – common criteria: popularity/virality, public interest, societal impact, timeliness; with variants like _attention-worthy_ and _harmful_). Mapped onto `ClaimItem`, this taxonomy exposes a naming drift: our `checkworthy` flag ("subjective/opinion/prediction → not check-worthy") actually encodes the survey's **verifiability**, not its check-worthiness – the field reserves "check-worthy" for the priority judgment our `relevanceScore` makes. Our `checkable` (evidence-modality reachability for provenance-type claims) is a distinct axis the survey folds into verifiability's evidence-availability caveat. And the survey's third factor, **similarity** – claim _matching_ against previously fact-checked claims plus claim _clustering_ to dedupe – names the task family the `claims:search` short-circuit belongs to (matching, which we do via Google's index) and the one we skip entirely (clustering: the segmenter is explicitly instructed "Do NOT merge near-duplicates", `lib/pipeline/segment.ts:27`, so repeated claims within one paste are each checked independently today). None of this forces a rename, but any external reporting should translate: our `checkworthy` ≈ their "verifiable", our `relevanceScore` ≈ their "check-worthiness/priority".

### 1.8 ClaimsKG

**What it encodes:** an RDF knowledge graph (~28k claims, 6.6M triples at publication) harvested from fact-checkers' ClaimReview markup, entity-linked to DBpedia, with claim coreference resolution and – the load-bearing part for us – **normalized truth ratings** across heterogeneous publisher scales (TRUE / FALSE / MIXTURE / OTHER), while keeping the original rating. Sources: Tchechmedjiev et al., ISWC 2019, [PDF](http://users.ics.forth.gr/~fafalios/files/pubs/ISWC2019_ClaimsKG.pdf); live graph at [data.gesis.org/claimskg](https://data.gesis.org/claimskg/). ClaimsKG is precedent for exactly the design our golds already use – keep `originalRating` verbatim, map to a coarse normalized enum, keep the mapping auditable (`evals/golden/schema.ts:39-41`) – and a cautionary tale for `ratingToStance`, which does the same normalization with a substring list and _without_ retaining anything but the raw string (`lib/factcheck.ts:58-99`).

### 1.9 MediaReview

**What it encodes:** a schema.org `Review` subtype for fact-checking **media items** (images/video/audio) rather than textual claims – `mediaAuthenticityCategory` drawing from `MediaManipulationRatingEnumeration` (_OriginalMediaContent, DecontextualizedContent, EditedOrSyntheticContent, ImitationContent, SatireOrParodyContent, StagedContent, TransformedContent_) plus `originalMediaLink`; linked from ClaimReview via `associatedMediaReview`. Still flagged as a new/in-progress definition on schema.org. Source: [schema.org/MediaReview](https://schema.org/MediaReview). VERITRACE has no media pipeline by design – this is precisely the `checkable: false` / `provenance` territory the golds stratify out (`evals/golden/schema.ts:82-84`). MediaReview matters to us only as the _name_ for that excluded category: if we ever emit ClaimReview, marking provenance-type claims as out-of-scope-because-MediaReview-territory is the standards-aligned way to say it.

### 1.10 IFCN

**What it standardizes:** _process_, not labels. The IFCN Code of Principles ([ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org](https://ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/)) binds signatories to transparency of sources, funding, and **methodology** (same standard and process for every check, evidence dictates conclusions), assessed against 31 criteria – but imposes **no shared rating vocabulary**; signatories keep their own heterogeneous scales ("half true", "three Pinocchios", "Distorcido"). There is no IFCN label taxonomy to map onto. The relevant alignment for VERITRACE is methodological: our stated-not-learned verdict rules (`lib/pipeline/verdict.ts:4-15`), the visible `QuestionTrace`, and the advisory rationale are the machine analogue of IFCN's methodology-transparency commitments, and IFCN signatory status is a reasonable future criterion for the curated `FACT_CHECKERS` trust list used at `lib/factcheck.ts:101-103`.

### 1.11 Stance detection

**What it encodes:** the position of a text toward a target. The canonical definition (Küçük & Can 2020, as adopted by the survey of record for the fact-checking use of stance – Hardalov et al., _A Survey on Stance Detection for Mis- and Disinformation Identification_, Findings of NAACL 2022, pp. 1259–1277, [arXiv:2103.00242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00242); vendored at `docs/papers/hardalov-2022-stance-detection-survey.pdf`): given a text–target pair, classify the author's stance as **Favor / Against / Neither** (sometimes plus Neutral). Label inventories vary by setting: FNC-1 uses _positive / negative / discuss / unrelated_ for article-vs-headline; the rumor-verification line (PHEME, RumourEval) uses **SDQC** – _Support / Deny / Query / Comment_ – for crowd responses. The survey distinguishes two roles: (a) **stance _as_ fact-checking**, where "the stance of the textual evidence(s) toward the target claim is considered as a veracity label", and (b) **stance as a component** of a larger verification pipeline. It also makes a distinction that matters for our field naming: FEVER-style evidence stance "slightly differs from typical stance detection, as it considers evidence supporting or refuting a claim, rather than the stance of an author towards a claim".

VERITRACE's 3-way `Stance` – `supports | refutes | contextualizes` (`lib/graph-types.ts:5`) – is the Favor/Against/Neither shape in the _evidence-stance_ (FEVER/FNC-1) sense, not the author-stance sense: it lives on `EvidenceItem` (`:20`), i.e., the survey's formulation (a), evidence stance as the veracity signal, feeding verdict prediction as formulation (b) prescribes. `contextualizes` plays the Neither/discuss/comment role, and `stanceConfidence` (`:23`) is a partial answer to the survey's "Label Semantics" complaint that standard inventories "do not capture the strength of the expressed stance". Two frictions worth naming. First, the same `Stance` type does double duty on `FactCheckHit` (`lib/factcheck.ts:38-39`), where what is being encoded is not a text's stance toward a claim but a _publisher's finished verdict_ re-projected onto the stance axis – in the survey's terms this is the _detecting previously fact-checked claims_ formulation ("assess the veracity of dubious content by evaluating the stance of a claim regarding already checked stories"), a related but distinct task. Second, `ratingToStance`'s rule "Ambiguous/mixed ⇒ contextualizes" (`lib/factcheck.ts:90`) overloads `contextualizes` with two different meanings – _merely discusses_ (FNC-1's discuss) and _mixed verdict_ (the survey's "Shades of Truth": half-true, mixture, and finer-grained scales) – which is the stance-axis face of the same normalization loss §3.1 flags for ratings. A "Mixture" rating is not a source that fails to take a position; it is a source that took a position our 3-way axis cannot represent.

## 2. Mapping VERITRACE onto each standard

### 2.1 Vocabulary crosswalk (per-claim labels)

| VERITRACE `Verdict`          | AVeriTeC                          | FEVER/FEVEROUS | FActScore     | SAFE          | ClaimsKG normalized | ClaimReview                                 |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `supported`                  | Supported                         | Supported      | supported     | supported     | TRUE                | free text (`alternateName`) + numeric scale |
| `refuted`                    | Refuted                           | Refuted        | not-supported | not-supported | FALSE               | free text + numeric scale                   |
| `conflicting`                | Conflicting Evidence/Cherry-pick. | –              | –             | –             | MIXTURE (roughly)   | "Mixture"/"Half true" style labels          |
| `nei`                        | Not Enough Evidence               | NotEnoughInfo  | –             | –             | OTHER (roughly)     | "Unproven"/"Unverified" style labels        |
| (dropped: `relevant: false`) | –                                 | –              | –             | irrelevant    | –                   | –                                           |

Divergence flags: (a) post-ADR-0007, `conflicting` is document-level only – VERITRACE can never emit it per-claim, while AVeriTeC golds contain it per-claim; (b) FActScore/SAFE's binary/3-way collapses our `nei` and `refuted` into "not-supported" – a FActScore-comparable number from a `ClaimTally` must be `supported / total`, treating NEI as failure, which is harsher than our UI's framing of NEI as abstention; (c) SAFE's "irrelevant" maps to our `dropped` count, which we correctly keep out of `total` (`lib/graph-types.ts:109-110`) exactly as SAFE keeps irrelevant facts out of F1@K.

### 2.2 Structural mapping (per standard)

| Standard       | Their unit                                      | Our unit                                                    | Alignment                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| FActScore/SAFE | generation → atomic facts → binary              | `SourceTextItem → ClaimItem[]` → 4-way                      | Same decompose-then-check shape; `ClaimTally` ≈ the FActScore numerator/denominator; `audit.ts` flags instead of auto-correcting (deliberate, documented)                      |
| ClaimReview    | one review of one claim                         | one resolved `ClaimItem` + evidence + rationale             | Near-lossless to emit (see §2.3); lossy to consume via `claims:search` (no numeric rating on the wire; we then substring-match the text)                                       |
| Google FCT API | `claims:search` hit list                        | `FactCheckHit` (`lib/factcheck.ts:26-42`)                   | Faithful to the wire format; the flattening to `Stance` happens in `ratingToStance` and discards "Mostly true" vs "True" granularity                                           |

| ClaimBuster    | sentence → CFS score [0,1]                      | `checkworthy` + `relevant` + `relevanceScore`               | We split their one scalar into three orthogonal booleans/scores; finer but not directly comparable                                                                             |
| ClaimsKG       | ClaimReview corpus → normalized enum + original | golds: `originalRating` + 4-way `Verdict`                   | Same keep-the-original-rating discipline in the golds; **absent** in the live `FactCheckHit`, which keeps `textualRating` but no normalized-rating audit trail beyond `Stance` |

No overlap in what we can check                                                                                               |

### 2.3 What speaking each natively would buy

**Emitting ClaimReview (JSON-LD per resolved claim)** – the highest-value export. A resolved `ClaimItem` maps nearly losslessly: `claimReviewed` ← `ClaimItem.text`; `itemReviewed` (a `Claim`) with `appearance` ← the source text and `datePublished` ← `ClaimItem.date`; `reviewRating.alternateName` ← our verdict label with `ratingValue` on a declared 4-point scale; `reviewBody` ← `rationale`; `citation`/`isBasedOn` ← the evidence URLs. Two honesty caveats: our verdict is _advisory and machine-generated_, and ClaimReview's ecosystem assumption is a human editorial process (IFCN-style) – an export should say so in `reviewBody` or an `author` `@type` that doesn't impersonate a human fact-checker; and `conflicting` only exists at source level, so a source-level ClaimReview (the whole paste as `itemReviewed`) is the natural place for it, with per-claim reviews carrying only supported/refuted/nei. What it buys: every downstream ClaimReview consumer (Data Commons, ClaimsKG-style harvesters, research tooling) can read a VERITRACE run; it is the honest interchange form of "advisory verdict + evidence trail". The June-2025 Search deprecation removes the SEO incentive but not the interchange value.

**Consuming ClaimReview structurally** – within `claims:search` the ceiling is low (no `ratingValue` on the wire), so the realistic upgrades are (a) replace the `ratingToStance` substring lists with a maintained normalization table that also records the _normalized rating_ (ClaimsKG-style TRUE/FALSE/MIXTURE/OTHER) on `FactCheckHit`, keeping the mapping auditable the way `GoldenSource.originalRating` already does; (b) for bulk/gold use, prefer the Data Commons feed, which carries full markup including numeric ratings – that is also the ADR 0002 escape hatch for post-2023 golds.

**FActScore-comparable reporting** – define and emit `supportRatio = supported / total` from `ClaimTally` alongside the categorical `sourceVerdict`, with the stated caveat that NEI counts against the ratio in FActScore's frame. Cheap (one derived number) and makes eval output citable against published FActScore baselines.

**AVeriTeC-score-style eval** – our golds already carry `questions[].keyEvidenceUrls`; grading verdict accuracy _conditioned_ on question/evidence match is implementable against `GoldenQuestion` and would align our eval headline number with the shared-task literature instead of bare verdict accuracy. The gate should be Ev2R-style (atomic-fact precision/recall by an LLM judge, §1.5), not the original hu-METEOR@0.25 – the AVeriTeC authors' own successor metric shows token matching is the weakest link precisely on real-world web evidence, and the alternative-evidence-paths failure mode it fixes is our default operating condition.

## 3. Open questions and recommended follow-ups

1. **Structured `FactCheckHit` normalization** (`lib/factcheck.ts`): add a `normalizedRating` field (TRUE/FALSE/MIXTURE/OTHER, ClaimsKG-style) recorded alongside `textualRating`, and move `ratingToStance` onto that normalization table – the current substring lists are the String-Parsing Smell the user-level instructions name, and every new publisher scale arrives as a new substring today.
2. **ClaimReview JSON-LD export**: one export function per resolved claim + one per source, with explicit machine-authorship marking. Decide the `Rating` scale declaration (`worstRating: 1, bestRating: 4`? ordinal order of nei/conflicting is ill-defined – possibly `alternateName`-only, which Google's guide permits since only `alternateName` is required).
3. **`supportRatio` in eval + UI reporting**: derive from `ClaimTally`; document the NEI-counts-against caveat.
4. **AVeriTeC-score harness**: implement the conditioned-accuracy metric over `GoldenQuestion.keyEvidenceUrls`, with an Ev2R-style judge (atomic-fact precision/recall, §1.5) as the evidence gate rather than hu-METEOR@0.25; the proxy-reference half of Ev2R (NLI confidence in the gold verdict given retrieved evidence) is implementable today from `(claim, verdict, evidence)` alone. Also decide the scoring rule for AVeriTeC golds labeled per-claim _Conflicting_ given the pipeline can never predict it (options: remap those golds to NEI per ADR 0007's reading, or stratify them out like provenance items).
5. **Monitor the ClaimReview corpus post-June-2025**: with Search rich results gone ([Google's notice](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/factcheck), [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/ifcn/2025/google-claimreview-fact-checks-snippets-removed/)), publisher markup volume may decay; the short-circuit's hit rate and the ADR 0002 escape hatch both depend on it. Track hit-rate in runs; keep the Data Commons feed URL as the fallback corpus.
6. **`relevanceScore` semantics**: decide whether it means check-worthiness (ClaimBuster's probability) or load-bearingness (its current doc comment) – today it drives capping only, but the moment we report it against external baselines the ambiguity becomes a comparability bug. Same for the flag names: per the claim-detection survey's taxonomy (§1.7), our `checkworthy` encodes what the field calls _verifiability_, and its "check-worthiness" is our `relevanceScore` – external reporting needs that translation stated.
7. **Abstention-bias profiling in eval**: the alignment-bottleneck result (§1.2) says sub-claim macro-F1 is a poor predictor of downstream verdict quality and that the commit/abstain and refute/affirm balance is what determines robustness – so the eval harness should report the pipeline's per-claim error _profile_ (NEI rate on verifiable golds, refutation recall/precision) alongside accuracy, and treat our NEI-leaning verdict rules as a stated bias with a named cost (missed refutations), not just a safety win.
8. **Keep retrieval sub-claim-aligned**: per-question-per-claim retrieval is the SAE condition under which decomposition actually pays (§1.2); treat any future shared-retrieval optimization (one search pass reused across claims) as a schema-level regression to SRE, to be justified against that result, not just against latency.
